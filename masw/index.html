We see it too often in the Laguna Creek corridor—a developer submits plans with a generic Site Class D assumption, only to have the building department flag it because the shear wave velocity data isn't local. Elk Grove sits on young alluvial deposits from the Cosumnes River system, and VS30 values here can swing from 180 m/s in the western floodplain to over 400 m/s near the Elliott Ranch paleochannels. Guessing wrong means overdesigning the lateral system or, worse, underestimating seismic demand. A proper MASW survey eliminates that gamble. We run the array, process the dispersion curve, and deliver a velocity profile that holds up under plan check scrutiny—something generic NEHRP maps simply cannot provide for the patchy soils of this basin.

A site-specific VS30 profile in Elk Grove often differs by 30% or more from the USGS proxy—enough to move a project from Site Class D to Site Class C and reduce base shear by 15%.

How we work

Last year we mobilized for a three-story medical office near Big Horn Boulevard. The geotech report showed interbedded silts and loose sands to about 14 meters, and the structural engineer needed a defensible VS30 for their ASCE 7 drift calculations. We laid out a 46-meter linear array with 4.5 Hz geophones right in the future parking lot, collected data across three shot points, and inverted the fundamental-mode dispersion curve using a layered half-space model. The result: 285 m/s average in the upper 30 meters, placing the site firmly in Site Class D but with enough granularity to justify a site-specific response spectrum—something the default mapped values couldn't capture. When liquefiable layers are suspected, we often pair this with CPT soundings to correlate tip resistance with shear wave velocity and refine the cyclic stress ratio estimates. For projects where deeper bedrock mapping matters, we've combined MASW with seismic refraction to constrain the velocity of the refusal layer beyond 30 meters, which helps when designing deep foundations near the Cosumnes River levee setbacks.

Local ground factors

Sacramento County has recorded over 80 felt earthquakes since 1900, and the USGS Unified Hazard Tool shows Elk Grove with a 10% probability of exceeding 0.25g peak ground acceleration in 50 years. The controlling source is the Coast Range-Sierran Block boundary zone, capable of generating a magnitude 6.5+ event at a distance of roughly 60 kilometers. What makes this relevant for shear wave velocity testing is the basin-edge effect: the sedimentary wedge underlying Elk Grove thickens westward, and impedance contrasts at the sediment-bedrock interface can amplify ground motion at periods that match mid-rise structures. A default VS30 from the USGS proxy map averages 270 m/s for this ZIP code, but our measured values on Franklin Boulevard sites have come in as low as 195 m/s—Site Class E territory. That difference triggers additional requirements under ASCE 7-22 Section 11.4.8 for site-specific hazard analysis, and missing it during design means the structural system may be unconservative by 20% or more in base shear demand.

Quick answers

How much does a MASW survey cost for a typical Elk Grove residential lot?

For a standard single-family residential parcel up to 7,500 square feet, our MASW survey with a 46-meter array, dispersion processing, VS30 calculation, and a signed report runs between US$1,750 and US$2,830. The final figure depends on site access, surface conditions (asphalt vs. compacted soil affects geophone coupling), and whether we need to run multiple arrays to avoid underground utilities. Commercial sites with larger arrays or combined active-passive acquisition fall toward the upper end of that range.

Does the City of Elk Grove accept MASW for building permit submittals?

Yes, the City of Elk Grove Building Division and most structural plan reviewers in Sacramento County accept MASW-derived VS30 profiles as long as the report follows ASCE 7-22 Chapter 20 methodology and includes the full dispersion curve, inversion parameters, and a statement of qualifications from the field geophysicist. We include the raw shot gathers and the shear-wave velocity model in the appendix so the reviewer can trace every processing step from acquisition to final VS30 value.

How deep can MASW investigate in the Elk Grove basin, and what happens if you hit refusal?

With a standard 46-meter active-source array and 4.5 Hz geophones, we typically resolve Vs down to 25–35 meters in Elk Grove's alluvial soils—enough for a reliable VS30. When we need to go deeper, we switch to a combined active-passive approach using ambient noise from traffic along Highway 99 or Laguna Boulevard, which extends the investigation to 50–60 meters. If the inversion shows a velocity exceeding 760 m/s at depth (rock or dense cemented gravel), that layer is recorded as engineering refusal and the profile is truncated there.

What is the difference between MASW and seismic refraction, and which one do I need for my project?

MASW measures shear-wave velocity (Vs) using surface-wave dispersion, giving us the parameter that directly feeds into ASCE 7 site classification and ground-motion analysis. Seismic refraction measures compressional-wave velocity (Vp) and is better at mapping bedrock depth and rippability. For seismic design in Elk Grove, MASW is the primary tool because VS30 drives the site class. Refraction becomes useful when you need to know the depth to the high-velocity refusal surface for deep foundation design—and the two methods work well together when combined on the same spread.
