We roll out the Syscal Pro with a 72-electrode spread, running both Wenner and Schlumberger arrays from the truck. In Elk Grove, where the alluvial fan meets basin deposits east of the Cosumnes River, we need the Schlumberger for deep vertical resolution—often past 100 feet—to separate the sand lenses from the clay plugs before a single bucket hits the ground. The Wenner array gives us the lateral edge, especially useful when scanning for paleochannel margins that show up as sharp resistivity lows.
For a typical half-acre lot off Laguna Boulevard, we can acquire four VES soundings and have the raw apparent resistivity curves calibrated with a borehole log from a nearby SPT drilling within a morning. The combination of direct push data and resistivity profiles cuts through the ambiguity of the shallow gravels that plague foundation design in the Laguna Creek drainage area. We process the inversion in Res2DInv right on site, so the excavator operator knows what to expect by noon.

Low resistivity zones below 15 ohm-m in Elk Grove almost always map the Modesto Formation clay—miss that contact and you're redesigning footings on site.

Methodology and scope

ASTM D6431-18 governs our field procedures—every spread is checked for contact resistance below 2 kOhm, and we run reciprocal measurements on at least 10% of the quadripoles to catch any cable leakage early. For Elk Grove, this matters because the dry summer hardpan north of Sheldon Road can drive contact resistance through the roof; we carry a water truck and salt-water electrodes for exactly those days.
The IBC references site-specific resistivity when evaluating corrosion potential for steel piles and when classifying expansive clay zones, both of which are relevant in eastern Sacramento County. When the resistivity drops below 15 ohm-m at 20-foot depth, we flag it for the structural engineer—that's usually the saturated clay layer that correlates with the Modesto Formation.
We often pair a single VES center point with a MASW survey on the same line to get both Vs30 and resistivity stratigraphy without mobilizing a drill rig, which saves a full day on reconnaissance-phase projects. For deeper targets—like mapping the gravel aquifer at 150 feet for a water supply well—we extend the AB/2 out to 300 meters and switch to a dipole-dipole array mid-spread to maintain resolution while pushing depth of investigation into the Mehrten Formation contact.

Local considerations

In Elk Grove we see a lot of projects where the geotech report only has SPT data from 25-foot borings, but the real problem is at 35 to 45 feet—a saturated sand lens that nobody logged. A single VES sounding with AB/2 extended to 200 feet would have spotted it as a resistivity high sandwiched between two conductive clay layers. That's artesian pressure waiting to surprise the dewatering contractor.
The other thing that catches people off guard is seasonal variation. Resistivity profiles run in October, after six months of zero rain and hardpan shrinkage, look nothing like the same profile run in February after the Laguna Creek basin has been recharging for weeks. The clay resistivity can drop by a factor of three. If you're using the resistivity to estimate corrosion class for steel piling—per Caltrans corrosion guidelines—you need the wet-season number, not the dry-season one. We always note the antecedent rainfall on the log header so the engineer can weight the data appropriately.

Associated technical services

01

1D Vertical Electrical Sounding (VES) for Stratigraphy

We set up a center-point spread and expand the current electrode spacing step by step, building a resistivity-versus-depth curve at a single location. In Elk Grove, this is the go-to method when you need to know the depth to the Modesto Formation clay or the thickness of the gravel aquifer under a proposed detention basin. We tie every sounding to a nearby boring log and deliver a calibrated layer model with true resistivity and thickness per layer.

02

2D Resistivity Imaging for Lateral Mapping

A multi-electrode cable laid along a 350-foot line gives us a continuous cross-section showing how resistivity changes laterally. This is what we use to trace paleochannel edges under the Cosumnes River floodplain, to find the transition from coarse fan deposits to fine basin deposits, or to map a clay seam that pinches out under a proposed building pad. Combined with CPT data, the 2D line becomes a powerful tool for interpolating between borings.

Frequently asked questions

What does a resistivity survey in Elk Grove actually cost?

For a typical residential or light commercial site in Elk Grove, expect between US$600 and US$1,080 for a package of four VES soundings with a basic interpretation report. The final number depends on AB/2 maximum spread—deeper investigation means longer cables and more time—and whether you need 2D imaging lines in addition to the point soundings. We'll give you a fixed price after we see the site and understand the target depth.

How deep can you see with electrical resistivity?

With a Schlumberger array and an AB/2 of 300 meters, we reach about 50 meters of investigation depth in Elk Grove's alluvial soils. That's enough to get through the Modesto Formation and into the Mehrten Formation contact. The practical limit is cable length and site access—on a tight infill lot we may be limited to an AB/2 of 100 meters, which still gives us 15 to 20 meters of penetration, plenty for most foundation investigations.

Do I still need borings if I do a resistivity survey?

Yes, absolutely. Resistivity gives you continuous vertical profiling and lateral coverage that borings alone cannot match, but it's an indirect method—it measures electrical properties, not soil type directly. We always calibrate the resistivity model against at least one boring with SPT or CPT data. Think of it this way: the boring tells you exactly what's at one point, and the resistivity tells you what happens between borings.

Can resistivity surveys work through asphalt or concrete?

Through asphalt, yes—we use coupling gel or drill small pilot holes for electrode contact on parking lots and roadways. Through reinforced concrete slabs, it's trickier because the rebar creates a conductive layer that shorts out the near-surface signal. On sites with heavy concrete cover, we shift to a roll-along array along the perimeter or use a seismic refraction line as an alternative that doesn't need direct ground contact.
